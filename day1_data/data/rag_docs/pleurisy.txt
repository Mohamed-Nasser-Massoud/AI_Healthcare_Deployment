Pleurisy (also called pleuritis) is inflammation of the pleura, the two thin layers of tissue that line the outside of the lungs and the inside of the chest cavity. Normally, a small amount of fluid between these layers allows them to slide smoothly against each other as the lungs expand and contract during breathing. When the pleura becomes inflamed, the layers can rub against each other roughly, causing sharp chest pain, especially when breathing deeply, coughing, or sneezing. Pleurisy has many possible causes, including viral infections (the most common cause), bacterial pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, autoimmune conditions like lupus or rheumatoid arthritis, and less commonly, lung cancer or tuberculosis. The hallmark symptom is a sharp, stabbing chest pain that typically worsens with breathing, coughing, or movement, and may improve when holding one's breath or lying on the affected side. Other symptoms can include shortness of breath, cough, and fever if an underlying infection is present. In some cases, fluid can accumulate in the space between the pleural layers, a condition called a pleural effusion, which can cause additional shortness of breath and may require drainage if significant. Diagnosis involves a physical exam (a doctor listening for a distinctive "rubbing" sound with a stethoscope), chest X-ray, and sometimes blood tests or imaging to identify the underlying cause. Treatment focuses on addressing that underlying cause, along with pain relief and anti-inflammatory medication to manage symptoms in the meantime.
