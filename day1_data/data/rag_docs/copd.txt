Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) is a long-term, progressive lung condition that makes it hard to breathe due to narrowed or damaged airways and air sacs. It's actually an umbrella term that mainly covers two related conditions: chronic bronchitis (long-term inflammation and mucus buildup in the airways) and emphysema (damage to the tiny air sacs in the lungs that reduces their ability to exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide efficiently). The overwhelming majority of COPD cases are caused by long-term exposure to irritants that damage the lungs, with cigarette smoking being the leading cause; long-term exposure to air pollution, chemical fumes, or dust can also contribute, especially in combination with smoking. Symptoms usually develop gradually and worsen over time, including chronic cough (often with mucus), wheezing, shortness of breath — especially during physical activity — chest tightness, and frequent respiratory infections. Unlike asthma, COPD-related airflow limitation is not usually fully reversible, though treatment can slow its progression and improve quality of life. There is no cure for COPD, but management typically includes quitting smoking (the single most impactful intervention), bronchodilator medications to relax airway muscles, inhaled corticosteroids to reduce inflammation, pulmonary rehabilitation programs, and supplemental oxygen therapy in more advanced cases. Flare-ups, called exacerbations, can be triggered by respiratory infections or air pollution and may require more intensive treatment.
