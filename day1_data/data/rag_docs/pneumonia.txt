Pneumonia is an infection that inflames the air sacs (alveoli) in one or both lungs, causing them to fill with fluid or pus. It can be caused by bacteria, viruses, or fungi, and the cause affects how it's treated: bacterial pneumonia is treated with antibiotics, while viral pneumonia typically resolves with supportive care since antibiotics don't work against viruses. Common symptoms include cough (often producing phlegm), fever, chills, difficulty breathing, chest pain that worsens with breathing or coughing, and fatigue. In young children, older adults, and people with weakened immune systems, pneumonia can become severe quickly and may require hospitalization. Diagnosis usually involves a physical exam (listening to the lungs), a chest X-ray to confirm fluid or consolidation in the lung tissue, and sometimes blood tests or sputum cultures to identify the causative organism. Risk factors include smoking, chronic lung disease, a weakened immune system, and recent viral respiratory infections like the flu. Vaccines exist for some of the most common bacterial causes (such as Streptococcus pneumoniae) and are recommended for young children, older adults, and people with certain chronic conditions. Someone with pneumonia should seek medical attention if they have difficulty breathing, persistent chest pain, a high fever, or confusion, as these can signal a more serious case.
