COVID-19, caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus, primarily affects the respiratory system, though it can impact other organs as well. It spreads mainly through respiratory droplets and aerosols released when an infected person breathes, talks, coughs, or sneezes. The severity of respiratory involvement varies widely: many infections cause mild, cold-like or flu-like symptoms, while others progress to viral pneumonia, and in severe cases, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), a life-threatening condition in which the lungs become severely inflamed and fill with fluid, drastically impairing oxygen exchange. Common respiratory symptoms include cough, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and in more severe cases, dangerously low blood oxygen levels that may not always correspond to how short of breath the person feels (a phenomenon sometimes called "silent hypoxia"). Risk factors for severe respiratory illness include older age, obesity, chronic lung disease (like COPD or asthma), heart disease, diabetes, and a weakened immune system. Diagnosis is typically confirmed through PCR or antigen testing, with chest imaging (CT or X-ray) sometimes used to assess the extent of lung involvement in more severe cases. Management ranges from supportive care (rest, fluids, fever reducers) for mild cases to supplemental oxygen, antiviral medications, corticosteroids to reduce lung inflammation, and mechanical ventilation in the most severe cases. Vaccination has been shown to significantly reduce the risk of severe respiratory illness, hospitalization, and death from COVID-19.
